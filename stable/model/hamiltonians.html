Hamiltonians ​

The pseudo-Hamiltonian of an optimal control problem depends only on the model: its dynamics , its Lagrange cost and its criterion. CTModels builds it, and the Hamiltonians derived from it, as methods of the generic functions CTBase.Data.pseudo_hamiltonian and CTBase.Data.hamiltonian, re-exported by CTModels.Models. CTFlows uses them to build the flows of the indirect method.

CallProblemReturns
pseudo_hamiltonian(ocp; p0)with a control
pseudo_hamiltonian(ocp; p0, constraints)with a control
hamiltonian(ocp, u; p0)with a control, control in feedback form
hamiltonian(ocp, u; p0, multipliers)with a control, control and multipliers in feedback form
hamiltonian(ocp; p0)control-free

Each object is callable with its natural signature, which follows the time and variable dependence of the problem (H̃(x, p, u) for an autonomous problem without variable, H̃(t, x, p, u, v) for a non-autonomous one with a variable), and with a uniform signature that takes every argument. A one-dimensional state, control, variable or multiplier is a number.

The pseudo-Hamiltonian ​

The example of this page is the problem with a state constraint of order one of Bonnard, Faubourg, Launay and Trélat: minimise for  ,  , under the constraint   .

julia
using CTModels
const Models = CTModels.Models

lb = 0.1
pre = CTModels.PreModel()
CTModels.time!(pre; t0=0.0, tf=2.0)
CTModels.state!(pre, 1)
CTModels.control!(pre, 1)
CTModels.dynamics!(pre, (r, t, x, u, v) -> (r[1] = u; nothing))
CTModels.objective!(pre, :min; lagrange=(t, x, u, v) -> x^2)
CTModels.constraint!(pre, :path;
    f=(r, t, x, u, v) -> (r[1] = x - lb; nothing), lb=[0.0], ub=[Inf], label=:x_con)
CTModels.constraint!(pre, :control; rg=1:1, lb=[-1.0], ub=[1.0], label=:u_box)
CTModels.time_dependence!(pre; autonomous=true)
CTModels.definition!(pre, quote end)
ocp = CTModels.build(pre)

H̃ = Models.pseudo_hamiltonian(ocp)
PseudoHamiltonian: autonomous, fixed (no variable)
  natural call: h̃(x, p, u)
  uniform call: h̃(t, x, p, u, v)
julia
x, p, u = 1.0, 2.0, 0.5
H̃(x, p, u)                     # natural call: p·u − x²
0.0
julia
H̃(0.0, x, p, u, nothing)       # uniform call H̃(t, x, p, u, v)
0.0

The pseudo-Hamiltonian is always maximised, for a minimisation and for a maximisation; only depends on the criterion (ct-conventions SGN4):

:min

So and   have the same pseudo-Hamiltonian, hence the same control, costate and multipliers. A whose sign contradicts the criterion is rejected.

julia
Models.pseudo_hamiltonian(ocp; p0=0)(x, p, u)        # abnormal case: p·u

    Models.pseudo_hamiltonian(ocp; p0=1)

p0 does not have the sign of the criterion: p0 = 1 for a :min problem

The law is a CTBase.Data.DynClosedLoop law, or a plain function with the natural arity of the problem: u(x, p), u(t, x, p), u(x, p, v) or u(t, x, p, v). On the first arc of the example,   :

julia
h = Models.hamiltonian(ocp, (x, p) -> -1.0)

-3.0

A plain function with a single method is checked against the arity of the problem; a function with several methods, or with a variable number of arguments, is not.

Constraints by label ​

The constraints active on an arc are adjoined by label, with their multipliers :

where each constraint    enters on its active side,  , and the multiplier is an explicit argument, like the control. This is the pseudo-Hamiltonian of the maximum principle with constraints.

julia
H = Models.pseudo_hamiltonian(ocp; constraints=:x_con)
ConstrainedPseudoHamiltonian: autonomous, fixed (no variable)
  natural call: H(x, p, u, μ)
  uniform call: H(t, x, p, u, μ, v)
julia
μ = 3.0
H(x, p, u, μ)                  # p·u − x² + μ (x − lb)
2.7

Several labels are given as a tuple; then stacks their multipliers in that order, each with the components of its constraint. Labels not given are inactive.

A constraint written as a box or as a path constraint has the same term, and the same multiplier (ct-conventions SGN5).

The bounds of a box are the effective bounds of its components: when a component is bounded under several labels, its bounds are intersected, and each of these labels gives the same box, and the same dual in the solution. With x₁(t) ≥ 0, (a) and x₁(t) ≤ 1, (b), the label :a gives the two-sided term of   , whose multiplier is signed:   when the lower bound is active. Adjoin one of the two labels; giving both would add the term twice, and is rejected.

A control box is inert in the flow: on an arc where the control is saturated,  , the term    vanishes, and so do its derivatives in and (with fixed, or with constant along the arc). Its multiplier is given by the stationarity   ; this is why it is usually left out, but adjoining it gives the dual of the control box, to compare with a direct method.

Each multiplier is a CTBase.Data.Multiplier, or a function with the natural arity of the problem: μ(x, p), μ(t, x, p), μ(x, p, v) or μ(t, x, p, v). Its value must have the dimension of its constraint (a number for a one-dimensional constraint).

Control-free problems ​

A problem without control has a Hamiltonian, and no pseudo-Hamiltonian:

julia
pre = CTModels.PreModel()
CTModels.time!(pre; t0=0.0, tf=1.0)
CTModels.state!(pre, 2)
CTModels.dynamics!(pre, (r, t, x, u, v) -> (r[1] = x[2]; r[2] = -x[1]; nothing))
CTModels.objective!(pre, :min; lagrange=(t, x, u, v) -> x[1]^2)
CTModels.time_dependence!(pre; autonomous=true)
CTModels.definition!(pre, quote end)
ocp_cf = CTModels.build(pre)

H_cf = Models.hamiltonian(ocp_cf)
H_cf([1.0, 2.0], [3.0, 4.0])   # p₁x₂ − p₂x₁ − x₁²
1.0

Constraints are not adjoined to the Hamiltonian of a control-free problem: such a problem is easily ill-posed, and these conditions are rather boundary conditions. pseudo_hamiltonian(ocp_cf) and hamiltonian(ocp_cf; multipliers=…) raise a CTBase.Exceptions.PreconditionError, as does hamiltonian(ocp) for a problem with a control, which needs a control law.

Batched states ​

The flows of CTFlows can integrate several points at once: the state is then a Matrix, one column per point, and so are the costate, the control, the variable and the multipliers, a quantity of dimension being a   batch matrix. The Hamiltonians accept such arguments, and their value is the sum over the points, , so that its gradient in the batched state is the gradient at each point, column by column.

The functions of the model then receive whole matrices: the dynamics, the Lagrange cost and the path constraints must be written on the columns. The dynamics and the path constraints fill one column per point of an   batch buffer, and the Lagrange cost returns the sum of its values over the points:

julia
dynamics!(r, t, x, u, v) = (r .= A * x .+ B .* u; nothing)
lagrange(t, x, u, v) = 0.5 * sum(abs2, u)                 # Σ over the points
path!(r, t, x, u, v) = (r[1:1, :] .= x[1:1, :] .+ u; nothing)

A box needs nothing more: its rows are taken from the batched state or control. A multiplier that is not batched (a number, or a vector of the dimension of the constraints) is shared by all the points. A batched multiplier must have one column per point; otherwise the call raises a CTBase.Exceptions.IncorrectArgument.
